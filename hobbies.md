<!-- <h1>The Personal Side</h1> -->
<h1>The In-Between</h1>

<p class="lead">
  I enjoy challenging myself, spending time outdoors, and making things
  with my hands. Whether I'm working my way up a climbing route, exploring
  with a film camera, or shaping a piece of clay, I'm always looking for
  new experiences and new things to learn.
</p>

<div class="cards">
  <div class="card">
    <h3>Rock Climbing</h3>
    <p>
      From outdoor bouldering to lead climbing, I love the mix of movement,
      problem-solving, and mental challenge. I'm always looking for new
      places to explore, pushing my limits, and learning to embrace
      the uncertainty that comes with stepping outside my comfort zone.
    </p>
  </div>

  <div class="card">
    <h3>Pottery & Crochet</h3>
    <p>
      I enjoy working with my hands and turning raw materials into
      something tangible. Pottery gives me space to experiment, embrace
      imperfections, and learn through trial and error, finding joy in
      the process of creating something entirely my own.
    </p>
  </div>

  <div class="card">
    <h3>Film Photography</h3>
    <p>
      I collect vintage analog cameras and enjoy capturing wildlife,
      friends, and everyday moments. Shooting on film encourages me
      to slow down, look more closely at the world, and appreciate
      the unpredictability of capturing a moment.
    </p>
  </div>
</div>


<!-- <h2>Comfortable with Discomfort</h2> -->
<h2></h2>

<p>
  I try to make room for experiences that challenge me and take me somewhere
  unfamiliar. Climbing has become one of my favourite ways to do that.
  Whether I'm figuring out a difficult move on a boulder, leading an
  outdoor route, or balancing on a highline, there's always a moment when
  I have to trust myself, accept uncertainty, and take the next step.
</p>

<p>
  I enjoy exploring new places and making the most of opportunities to
  get outside. For me, these experiences are about more than the activity
  itself: they're a way to challenge my fears, learn about myself, and
  collect memories along the way.
</p>

<!-- <h2>A Different Kind of Focus</h2> -->
<h2></h2>

<p>
  When I'm not studying or climbing, I enjoy activities that let me
  create something with my hands. Pottery and film photography offer
  a different pace from the digital world: one is about shaping and
  experimenting with materials, while the other is about observing
  and capturing moments.
</p>

<p>
  I like that neither process is entirely predictable. A piece of pottery
  might turn out differently than expected, and a roll of film always
  holds a little uncertainty until it is developed. Both remind me to
  enjoy the process rather than focus only on the result.
</p>
